# GLP-1 Pilot — Standard Replies for Clinical Questions from Non-Patients

Reference for checklist item **5.3** in `LAUNCH_CHECKLIST.md`. Two standard
replies and the rule for choosing between them. Recorded 2026-08-06.

## Which reply to use

**Reply 1 is for questions. Reply 2 is for anyone describing how they feel.
When in doubt, Reply 2.**

The failure mode being guarded against is sending a template about prescribers
to someone describing acute symptoms at nine at night.

## Reply 1 — the standard one

For questions about their own numbers, their medication, or whether something
is a problem.

> Thanks for asking rather than guessing.
>
> I have to be straight about what I can and can't do here. This program is a
> tracking service rather than medical care, and you're not my patient, so I
> can't advise you on your medication, your symptoms, or whether a particular
> reading is a problem for you. That isn't a technicality I'm hiding behind.
> Answering those questions without knowing your history, your labs and
> everything else you take is how people get hurt.
>
> The person to ask is whoever prescribes your GLP-1. They have your history
> and they can act on the answer. If it feels urgent, don't wait for an
> appointment.
>
> What I can help with is the program itself. What your protein target means,
> why we measure glucose the way we do, how to read the pattern in your own
> numbers, anything about how the app works. Ask me any of that and I'll
> answer properly.
>
> If you'd rather have a clinical conversation with me specifically, that
> means becoming a patient and I can tell you how that works. But for most
> questions your own prescriber is the better answer, not the fallback.
>
> Dr. Chad Larson

**Why Reply 1 points at the prescriber before offering a consult:** the
prescriber already has the relationship, the history and the ability to act,
and leading with "become my patient" in response to a health worry reads as an
upsell.

## Reply 2 — for anyone describing symptoms

> I'm not able to assess symptoms over email, and I don't want you waiting on
> me while you're feeling like this.
>
> If it's severe or getting worse, go to urgent care or an emergency room now.
> Don't wait for a call back from anyone, including your prescriber.
>
> If it's new or unusual but not that bad, call whoever prescribes your GLP-1
> today rather than at your next appointment. Tell them what you told me.
>
> Write back when you're sorted and let me know how you got on.
>
> Dr. Chad Larson
